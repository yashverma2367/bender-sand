ANIME CLASH · DEMO

1 · Hit-stop + impact frame. The moment they touch, time freezes for a few frames and the screen flips to a stark, high-contrast negative for 1–2 frames, then a white flash. This is the "impact frame" made famous by One Piece (Wano / Gear 5): split-second frames with exaggerated contrast that sell the hit.

2 · Clash sphere + tug of war. In a beam struggle (Goku vs Frieza style) the contact point becomes a pulsing ball of light. It wobbles back and forth as each side pushes, the losing beam thins, and the winner's beam thickens.

3 · Energy splash. The two energies can't pass each other, so they spray off the contact point sideways in sharp, tapered tongues, each in its owner's color, like water off a rock.

4 · Lightning arcs. Jagged arcs crackle around the sphere while the struggle lasts, flickering every couple of frames.

5 · Focus lines. Manga-style speed lines rush in from the edges toward the clash, pulling your eye to it.

6 · Rising debris + cracks. The ground under the clash cracks and blocky rocks float up from the pressure (the Dragon Ball aura cliché; chunky, cube-like debris is a Yutaka Nakamura signature).

7 · Release: shockwave rings. When one side breaks through, rings expand from the point, flattened like ellipses (drawn in perspective, as if lying on the ground), with a pillar of light, a screen shake and debris blasted outward.

8 · Cel-shaded dust. Anime dust isn't a soft fog: it's chunky clouds with hard-edged 2–3 tone shading that billow out and roll, then thin away, with embers and smoke in the afterglow.
